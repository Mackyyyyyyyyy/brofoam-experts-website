Why blown-in?

Insulation Added Without Demolition

Blown-in is the practical option when the building is already finished. The material is fed through a hose and fills the cavity from the inside, so there is no need to strip back drywall or lift finished flooring to get coverage where it is missing.

It settles around wiring, pipework and bracing rather than being interrupted by them, which is exactly where batt material tends to leave gaps in an older structure.

What is your attic costing you?

Attic Coverage That Cuts Heat Loss at the Source

An underinsulated attic is the single biggest heat loss in most Michigan homes. Warm air rises and escapes through the ceiling all winter, which drives up the heating bill and feeds ice dams on the roof.

Bringing the attic up to a proper depth flattens that loss. Most people notice it first in the rooms directly underneath, which stop being several degrees colder than everywhere else.

Benefits of Blown-In Insulation

Continuous coverage

Loose fill flows around ducting, wiring and bracing, avoiding the gaps that undermine a batt install in an older attic.

No demolition required

Added from the inside through a hose, so there is no need to open up finished walls or ceilings to get coverage.

Sound dampening

The same loose fill that slows heat loss also absorbs airborne sound under a roof or between floors.
